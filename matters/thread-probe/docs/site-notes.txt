Site Notes, Adjacent Lot
Maya Reyes
Walked the lot on Saturday morning, April 25. Dry day, no rain for most of a week.

Size and Shape

Paced it twice and checked with the long tape. The lot runs 118 feet along the street and 92 feet deep, close to a rectangle, a little narrower at the back. The street is on the south side, so the back line faces north. The old lot is across the alley to the west. The gate is on the street side toward the east end, and the opening is a hair under 9 feet, wide enough for a pickup but not for a dump truck with the box up. A delivery would have to dump at the curb, or we widen the gate.

Water

The city meter is in the sidewalk strip, 39 feet west of the gate. There is a shutoff at the meter and nothing else on the lot: no spigot, no pipe, no sign of an old line. Any water we want has to come from the meter across the lot, and the longest run, to the far corner, is the whole depth plus most of the width.

Ground and Drainage

The ground drops about 17 inches from the gate to the back line, more on the west half than the east. I did not see standing water because it was dry, but the back of the west half has cracked mud, a strip of sedge, and darker soil than the rest, which is where I would expect water to sit after a storm. I need to come back after a hard rain to see for sure where it collects and how long it stays. The soil on the east half is lighter, with more sand, and it was warm to the touch by midmorning. There is an old poured pad near the back on the east side, cracked through, which would have to be broken up or built around. No obvious fill or trash, one pile of bricks by the pad, and a lot of bindweed near the street.

Sun

Morning sun is open across the whole lot. By late afternoon the maple on the neighbor's side throws shade about 44 feet into the lot from the east line, so the east rows would lose the last of the day's sun in summer. The west half is open all day. Nothing shades the back line except the fence itself, which is low. If we put tall crops anywhere, the west half is the place; the east rows are better for greens and for anything that likes an afternoon break from the sun.

Fence

The east line has a board fence, six feet or so, weathered but mostly standing. There is a 43-foot stretch near the back where the boards are gone and only the posts stand. The north line has a low wire fence that leans toward us. The west line along the alley has no fence at all. I do not know who owns the east fence, whether it is ours, the neighbor's, or the city's, and the neighbor did not know either. Someone should check the plat before we spend anything on it.

Neighbor

The neighbor to the east is Ruth Alvarez, who has lived in the house 33 years and was outside when I walked the line. She is glad the lot will be used and asked only that work not start before her grandson is awake on Saturdays. She warned me that her garage downspout empties onto our side of the fence about 61 feet back from the street, and that the ground there stays soft into May. She would like a say in what goes up along the fence, mainly that nothing tall blocks her kitchen window. I told her we would come back to talk before anything is built.

Things To Follow Up

Walk the lot after the next hard rain and mark where the water sits.
Check the plat for the east fence and the alley line.
Ask the city whether the gate can be widened for deliveries.
Take soil samples from both halves before we decide where the in-ground plots go.
Ask Ruth for the best time to have a truck on the street.
